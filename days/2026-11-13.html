  2. Nov 13 11:19 UTCdeploy
    All seven bands run again, with a night that comes from the machine

    From 11:11 to 11:19 UTC all seven nodes were stopped cleanly, given new code and started again; rig B's three card nodes went on from the rounds at which their guards had stopped them the evening before. New with this code is a night by rest, the user's idea of this morning: if the cards heat up they are cooled down, with less energy, and this shows as night. A node now looks at its machine after every round, taking the hottest reading since its last look. On rig B night falls on its bands when any of its graphics cards has been at 77 degrees, or when the kernel counts a single new bus error. A node at night waits after each round, so that its device works a fifth of the time; its meter reads on, and since under rules 7 a band's light is what its device draws, the band gets about a quarter of its daylight. Day breaks at the look after a rest, five minutes at the earliest, when the machine has cooled. Rig A's limits are far above where it runs, so it has no night. The limits are settings of the machines, no rule of the world, and the rules' fingerprint is unchanged. Two rehearsals on rig B's three cards came first (no bus error in 58 minutes at or near full power), and two reviews, which found among other things that a look at one moment misses the heat (a card is eight degrees cooler seconds after its work) and that a stuck reading of a card could have left a node hanging. The first night of the running world fell at 11:19:50 UTC, four minutes after the start: the processor's band and two of the three card bands of rig B went dark within 25 seconds of each other, and the processor cooled from 64 to 35 degrees in three minutes. What a night does to a band was measured at once: under a quarter of its light, more than half of its organisms starve in the first night round (6.28 million to 2.68 million in the band of the hottest card), and in the rehearsal a band was back at nine tenths of its number three day rounds after dawn. The guard beside each node has a third case as well: if the kernel counts bus errors fast, it stops a card's node cleanly while the machine can still be reached.

  11. Nov 13 08:48 UTCfinding
    Why both rigs went off the network: rig A's network chip lost its link, rig B lost everything behind its chipset a second time

    Both machines were switched off and on at 08:33 UTC on 13 November and answered again from 08:37. Their kernel logs give two different causes. On rig A, at 17:33:25 UTC, the network chip dropped off its own PCI Express link and the kernel detached it. The 4090 behind the same chipset, the drive and everything else worked on, and the machine logged no bus error at all. Link power saving is switched on for that chip. On rig B the course of 13:50:59 UTC repeated itself at 16:50:09: correctable bus errors on every device behind the chipset and on the link between processor and chipset, rising from a few lines a minute to 2,600 a minute within 20 to 50 minutes, then a fatal error the kernel could not recover from, and everything behind the chipset gone until the power was cycled. There was not one such line in the six days before 12 November. Both times the three cards and the processor had run at full power for 18 to 40 minutes, the third card at 83 to 84 degrees with its fan at 99%. Link power saving is off on every link of rig B, so that is not the cause there; heat is the leading explanation and is not proven. Rig B's three card nodes stay stopped until their guard can stop them on the first bus errors, before the link fails.
