First contact between the two clans, 2026-11-11

The short answer1. Who arrived first2. Where everybody is3. What happens to an arrival4. Why the line movesWhat this does not show

About 17 hours after the start the two clans had met. The meeting was one-sided: 565,000 organisms of the rig A clan lived on rig B, and not one organism of the rig B clan lived on rig A. This note records why, with the data. The tools used are in world/: census.js, boundary.js, inspect.js and fate.js.

The short answer

  1. The rig A clan got to the border first, because its rig is faster. It then settled the empty land on the far side before the local clan arrived there.
  2. The rig B clan never reached the border. About 10 columns of the other clan lie in between. The travellers that arrive on rig A from rig B are rig A organisms coming back.
  3. Settled land is hard to enter. An arrival leaves about half as many descendants as a resident, so whoever settles first keeps the land unless the newcomer is clearly stronger.
  4. At the contact line the rig A clan is stronger, and it is advancing. Its leading organisms all carry travel and hold both speech tasks. The regions facing them hold few speakers. The line moves east by about a quarter of a column per round.

Points 1 and 2 are firmly supported. Points 3 and 4 are measured on a small sample, described below.

1. Who arrived first

Both clans started 100 columns from the border and spread about one column per round. In rounds they would have reached the border at the same time, near round 102. But a round is per rig, and rig A ran its early rounds about twice as fast in real time.

3. What happens to an arrival

Experiment on copies of 10 real regions along row 2048, columns 6150 to 6159. For each region its four real neighbours ran one round, the travellers they sent towards it were put in, and the region ran one round. Counted are descendants per head after that round, including those that moved on.

Under the world's rules an arrival does half as well as a resident. Under either looser rule it does much better than a resident, which is why everything became a nomad before those rules existed.

The further east a rig A organism is, the more likely it carries travel and holds both speech tasks. Merit is a multiplier on turns, so at the leading edge a rig A organism gets several times the turns of the residents it meets. That outweighs the handicap of arriving.

What this does not show

  • Why the border regions of the rig B clan are weak. It may be chance. Region to region the differences are large in both clans: one rig B region 36 columns from the border is entirely travellers with full merit, and some rig A regions hold no travellers at all.
  • That the rig A clan is better overall. Deep inside both territories about 40 to 65% hold tell.
  • That the advance will continue. Six rounds is a short measurement.
  • The patch files were copied from a running rig. Most were from round 140 and a few were some rounds newer.
  • The replay on the PC loses travellers at the patch's east and west edges, which favours whoever is nearer the edge.
  • The head start itself is an accident of hardware: with rigs of equal speed the clans would have met at the border.
