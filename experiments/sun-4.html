What it is

The same world on the same two rigs, started again from one plant and one grazer, under rules 6. Under rules 5 a correct answer to a task still paid a little energy. Under rules 6 nothing is paid: energy enters as light, and moves on by eating. An organism can rest, give energy away, graze and leave a mark; it can sense hunger, what lies near it, scent, room, warmth, dusk, the season, its own age and guard, and it pays for each look.

The new senses and acts sit behind two instructions. in reads one of twelve senses, out does one of five acts (rest, give, graze, mark, name), and the nops that follow say which. Every organism shows a word, a number from 0 to 15 that it inherits from its mother: say calls it out, hear hears a call nearby, and nothing in the rules says what a word means.

No founder uses any of this, so it has to appear by mutation. Both founders catch light twice before anything else: under rules 5 a newborn's first meal was the pay for a task, and that is gone.

How it was started

The owner ordered it on 2026-11-12. The third start was stopped cleanly after 93 minutes and kept. Before the start: the two engines agreed on rules 6 in every mode, the tests and 52 cheat attempts passed on the definition, a graphics card on each rig gave the same world as the CPUs, a small connected world ran across both rigs, and both founders took hold on the very regions they start from. Not done before the start: the run on many seeds was made on the definition as it stood one step earlier, not on the final one.

How it will be read

Against the third start, number by number and round by round: the charts hold the third start's lines beside this one's. The third start lasted 93 minutes, so the comparison is short. The claims are judged anew for this start.

    Why rig B went off the network: its mainboard lost the link to its chipset

    Rig B is back since 14:55 UTC, after being switched off and on. Its kernel log gives the cause: at 13:50:59 UTC the mainboard lost the link to its chipset, a PCI Express error that the kernel could not recover. Both network ports, the wireless card, USB and SATA are behind the chipset and went with it. The processor, the memory, the three graphics cards and the drive are not, and kept working. So rig B's node ran on without a network for an hour, to round 348 with 175,574 inhabited regions and 63.9 million organisms, and nobody could reach it to stop it cleanly. What was in its memory at the switch-off is gone; its drive holds its regions as of round 167. There was no bus error in the six days before and none since the restart. The earlier entry said its last reading was at round 293: that was the last one the tracker could fetch. To do: a guard on each rig that stops the node cleanly when the network has been gone for some minutes.
