Question

Does the soup of the earlier single-machine runs keep evolving when it becomes one large world, with room to spread, two separate origins and a border that runs over the network between two machines?

Setup

  • A torus of 6,784 by 4,096 regions, each a 64 KB soup. Rig A holds columns 0 to 6143, rig B columns 6144 to 6783.
  • Two new instructions: travel moves an organism to a neighbouring region, look tells how crowded one is. No founder contains travel; it has to appear by mutation.
  • Every inhabited region runs a fixed 1,000,000 instructions per round. Nothing is paid for, so activity is free.
  • One plain ancestor per rig, 100 columns from the shared border. Each organism carries its clan: 0 from rig A, 1 from rig B.

What happened

  • travel appeared within the first rounds on both rigs and the two populations spread out as diamonds around their origins.
  • First contact, after about 17 hours. The clan from rig A reached the border first, because its rig is faster, and settled the empty land beyond it. At the stop 620,680 of the 17.9 million organisms on rig B came from rig A, and none from rig B lived on rig A. Settled land proved hard to enter: an arrival left about half as many descendants as a resident.
  • Rounds grew long as the world grew: the last round on rig A took 34 minutes.

Why it ended

It was stopped cleanly to make room for the sun world, which adds light, stored energy, eating and death. The data is kept, and the run can be resumed from the tag.
