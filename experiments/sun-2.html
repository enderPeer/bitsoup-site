Question

The same as in the first start, under rules that keep an honest ledger: does an ecology of plants, eaters, scavengers and parasites form and last? And does the world stay alive as it grows past what the two machines hold in memory?

What changed from the first start

  • A body pays out exactly what its mother spent building it, at most 12 flips per byte.
  • Nobody pays more than it has: a turn ends when the reserve is empty.
  • look sees only the living.
  • A round always has 64 passes. A rig that writes too much to its disk waits in real time instead.
  • Old age: an organism dies after spending 200,000 flips in all, so a region where nothing is born still turns over.

How it runs

Rig A computes with 12 CPU threads and two graphics cards, rig B with 24 CPU threads and three. Every engine gives the same bytes, so the world does not depend on which one steps a region. The engine was replaced while the world ran: by C compiled to WebAssembly at 13:39 UTC, by C on the graphics cards at 14:39 UTC, and by a RAM tier for regions that do not fit at 16:04 UTC. Each change was a clean stop and start, except one crash at 14:36 UTC after which both nodes rebuilt their state from the region files. The journal below lists every stop.

First observations

  • The two clans met. By 15:22 UTC 1.8 million organisms of the clan from rig A lived on rig B, and none of the other clan on rig A, the same one-sided contact as in the first world.
  • Populations swing with day and night: more than half of all organisms starve every night.
  • On rig A, which started from a plant only, plants that also eat made up 16% of all organisms at 15:22 UTC; on rig B, which started from a grazer, 45%.

  26. Nov 11 22:05 UTCdeploy
    Travellers deflated and let go after sending; both nodes started again (6937102)

    The cause of both crashes: since 9044974 the batches of travellers were the engine's records, not deflated. At dawn one round's travellers came to about 10 GB on rig A, and the node's main process held them two to three times over. The fix: the workers deflate the batches (5.1 times smaller, measured), and the main process lets go of its copy of the arrivals as soon as they are sent. The cards get a 384 KB arena for leavers, because the dawn give-backs (up to 13,730 regions in a round on one card) all came from the 128 KB arena running full; their slots are reduced to fit, to 57,000 and 25,000 regions on rig A and 25,000 on each of rig B's three cards. Checked with the world's tests, the link journal's checks and the node check on the RTX 4090 with the new arena. Both rigs' workers stored every region when their main processes died (all 283,527 of rig B's region files were rewritten), so the start reads them all first, as after any unclean stop, and then goes on: little or nothing of the world is lost. RUNTIME watches both rigs through the next dawn.

  35. Nov 11 15:44 UTCdecision
    No limit on land - the memory and disk use of the world are to be optimised instead

    The world grows by more than a thousand regions a minute and had reached what the two rigs hold in video memory and RAM. Of the three ways out (a limit on land, larger write budgets, tighter memory) the choice was tighter memory.
